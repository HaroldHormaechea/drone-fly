# Use Case 58: Takeoff-oriented reward redesign — eliminate the early-termination trap

## Summary
The drone-fly plant is now flight-capable — UC-55 added the inner-loop rate controller (clean, non-tumbling attitude) and UC-56 retuned the nominal to a Meteor75 Pro analog with a healthy T/W of 2.5 — but the reward function, accreted across ~15 use-cases while the drone could not fly, now **incentivizes early termination rather than flight**. Analysis of the fresh UC-55+UC-56 training run (recordings episodes 0–2450) shows: cumulative reward *rises* as episode length *falls* (Pearson correlation −0.87 between total reward and frame count — the two shortest 12-frame episodes scored the best), commanded throttle *declines* over training (mean 0.53 → 0.30, correlation −0.70 with training progress), every airborne-start episode descends to the floor (z-end ≈ 0.01 m from a 1.0 m start), and gate success stays flat at 0%. The policy is optimizing to end episodes as fast as possible — hitting the ground stops the per-step `time_penalty` bleed — because it can earn no positive reward (no gate is reachable and there is no effective climb incentive). This use case **redesigns the reward so that sustained flight and altitude gain strictly dominate early termination**, with the near-term optimization target being **take off and hold altitude** (gate/completion remaining the top of the objective hierarchy). Per the owner's direction, the redesign is **from scratch at the analyst's discretion** — a coherent reward built from first principles is preferred over patching the existing tangle — and it **reconciles/retires the overlapping legacy machinery** (`time_penalty`, the UC-39 crash-cliff `collision_penalty` override, UC-25 grounded/no-progress early-termination, and the `altitude_hold_*` / `climb_*` / `ground_break_*` terms). The new reward becomes the **default for fresh runs**. Work centers on `src/drone_fly/env/reward.py` and the reward config, plus the termination/penalty coupling in `src/drone_fly/env/racing_env.py`. All tests are hermetic; the behavioral takeoff verdict is deferred to the owner's fresh GPU retrain.

## Acceptance Criteria
1. **Anti-suicide invariant:** on hermetic synthetic trajectories, a rollout that descends to the ground or terminates early yields **strictly lower** cumulative reward than an otherwise-identical rollout that holds or gains altitude over the same-or-longer horizon — i.e., the observed reward-vs-episode-length correlation (−0.87) is removed or inverted.
2. **No existence-penalty dominance:** ending an episode early by contacting the floor is never net-positive versus staying airborne — any terminal/ground cost is ≥ the per-step penalty saved by terminating early (asserted hermetically).
3. **Takeoff gradient:** leaving the ground and gaining altitude toward the target earns positive reward **before** any gate is reached, producing a smooth ground → airborne → hold signal (a climbing-from-floor trajectory scores strictly above a stays-grounded one).
4. **Climb rewarded but non-farmable:** hovering or oscillating to farm a per-step bonus does **not** exceed the reward of genuine upward progress (guards against the UC-50 loiter trap) — tested on stationary vs. climbing vs. loitering trajectories.
5. **Objective hierarchy preserved:** passing a gate / completing the course remains the dominant positive reward (takeoff/hold shaping is subordinate to traversal).
6. **Reconcile/retire legacy machinery:** the overlapping terms (`time_penalty`, UC-39 crash-cliff override, UC-25 early-termination, `altitude_hold_*`, `climb_*`, `ground_break_*`) are folded into or removed from the new reward so no duplicate or dead incentive paths remain; each removal/retirement is documented with rationale.
7. **From-scratch redesign permitted:** the implementation MAY replace the reward composition wholesale (not merely re-tune coefficients); the resulting reward is internally coherent and its terms documented.
8. **Default-on + configurable:** the new reward is the **default for fresh runs**, is deterministic, and its weights/mode are exposed and validated in the train and evaluate YAML (UC-51 exposure pattern).
9. **Documentation:** the README reward-table (repo convention) is updated to reflect the new reward terms and values.
10. **Hermetic-only:** every new/updated test runs without GPU, pybullet, or training; the behavioral takeoff verdict is explicitly deferred to the owner's fresh GPU retrain.

## Potential Pitfalls & Open Questions
- **Risk** — Loiter/farming trap (UC-50): a naive alive/altitude bonus re-introduces reward farming; the design likely needs potential-based (telescoping) shaping to stay non-farmable (AC4).
- **Risk** — γ coupling: `climb_gamma` must track the training `gamma` (UC-39); any potential-based shaping in the redesign must preserve correct telescoping and not silently change the effective discount.
- **Risk** — Because the new reward is default-on and legacy terms are retired, the reward changes for every run: the existing reward-based test surface (and README reward-table) will churn substantially — expected, and consistent with the mandated fresh retrain (old checkpoints do not transfer).
- **Assumption** — The early-termination trap is the dominant cause; the analyst should confirm against `reward.py` exactly which term(s) drive the −0.87 reward-vs-length correlation before committing to the redesign.
- **Edge case** — The reward must produce takeoff **from the floor** (spawn_z → 0, the last curriculum stage), not merely altitude-hold from a 1 m airborne start; the takeoff gradient (AC3) must hold at ground level.
- **Edge case** — Interaction with the airborne-start reverse curriculum (UC-44/UC-51) and the grounded early-termination rule (UC-25): the redesign must remain coherent as the spawn anneals from airborne to floor.

## Original Description
Derived from this session's diagnostic analysis of the fresh UC-55+UC-56 training run. The owner observed the drone "flies as a parabola, cleaner, to the ground" and asked to fix it. Recording analysis (episodes 0–2450) proved the plant is now healthy (T/W 2.5, clean attitude from the rate loop) but the policy never climbs and never scores a gate: total reward is negatively correlated with episode length (−0.87, shorter = better), commanded throttle declines over training (0.53 → 0.30), and every airborne-start episode ends on the floor — a classic early-termination / "suicide" reward trap where the per-step time-penalty makes ending the episode quickly the optimal strategy. The owner directed: define a use case to fix the reward so the drone is incentivized to take off and stay up, and **explicitly allow ideating a reward policy from scratch instead of adjusting the current ones if that is better** (the existing terms were repeatedly "messed around with" while the drone couldn't fly). This is a reward-shaping problem, not a controls or dynamics problem — UC-57 (control frequency) will not address it.
