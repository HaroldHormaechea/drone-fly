# Use Case 39: Takeoff bootstrap — relieve the crash cliff + dense upward-progress reward

## Summary
Across UC-37 (floor start + airborne survival reward) and UC-38 (decouple no-progress/timeout cut from the crash penalty) the "no learning signal" pathology was fixed and hovering was made net-positive, yet training still converges to a non-flying policy: the drone spawns, sits, and is cut by the no-progress detector at ~100 steps (`ep_len`≈101, `success` 0%, `ep_rew`≈−5 after UC-38). Diagnosis, from PPO's credit assignment: the sit-vs-hover *terminal* ordering is already correct (hover ≈ +5 > sit ≈ −5), so the blocker is **not** that sitting is too comfortable — it is that **every path from sitting to hovering runs through a genuine floor/ceiling collision (`collision_penalty`=100)**, and PPO propagates that −100 (discounted by γ) back onto the "throttle up" actions that begin any takeoff attempt. Those actions therefore receive negative advantage despite being correct, so the policy learns "attempting flight leads to disaster — don't." The genuine −100 crash cliff is the dominant barrier; merely penalizing the grounded state (e.g. a flat −1/tick) does not remove those crashes and, because the stuck detector caps a sitting episode at ~100 steps, would recreate ≈−105 and spawn a *suicide* optimum (a −100 crash that ends the episode becomes preferable to bleeding out on the ground). This UC attacks the barrier directly with two coupled levers: **(a) relieve the crash cliff during learning** — reduce the genuine collision penalty (a fixed lower value, or a curriculum that starts low while the drone learns to fly and ramps up for precision), kept high enough that a valid completion still strictly beats a floor-shortcut (UC-03) and genuine crashes still terminate and are penalized; and **(b) add a small, potential-based dense upward-progress reward** that pays immediately for gaining altitude toward a hover height (telescoping so a round trip nets ≈0 and bobbing cannot be farmed into a loiter optimum). Together these make the first increments of flight net-positive in expected advantage instead of punished, without breaking the reward orderings established in UC-03/16/37/38. The exact mechanism and magnitudes (fixed reduction vs training curriculum; climb-reward weight and target height) are the analyst/challenger's call, pinned by tests. This deliberately changes default training dynamics; airborne-crash termination semantics and determinism are preserved.

## Acceptance Criteria
1. **Dense upward-progress (climb) reward (default on).** A new, documented, configurable per-step reward provides positive signal for upward progress from a floor start toward a target hover altitude, paid on the step the altitude is gained (before and independent of any later crash). A test asserts: a step that increases altitude toward the target yields a positive climb-reward contribution; a step resting on the floor yields ≈0; a step above the target height yields ≈0 additional climb reward (capped — no incentive to climb into the ceiling).
2. **Not farmable / no loiter optimum.** The climb reward is potential-based (telescoping) or otherwise constructed so that (a) a round trip — climb then descend by the same amount — nets ≈0 climb reward, and (b) the maximum total climb reward accruable over any single episode is strictly bounded below `completion_bonus` (and ideally at/below a single normalized `gate_bonus`). A test asserts the round-trip ≈0 property and the per-episode bound.
3. **Crash-cliff relief.** The genuine floor/ceiling/OOB collision penalty applied during training is reduced from the current 100 — either a fixed lower default or a training-time curriculum (low early, ramping up) — chosen so that a climb-then-crash trajectory is no longer catastrophically worse than sitting (the expected advantage of attempting takeoff is not dominated by the crash term). The shipped value/curriculum is documented and pinned by a test. If a curriculum is used, a test asserts the schedule endpoints and monotonicity; if a fixed value, a test asserts it.
4. **Genuine crashes still penalized and terminating.** Whatever value/curriculum is chosen, a genuine floor/ceiling/OOB collision still applies a negative penalty and still terminates the episode (`terminated is True`, `info["collided"] is True`). The UC-38 decoupling is preserved: no-progress/timeout cuts remain penalty-free. Tests assert both.
5. **Completion dominates loitering.** The maximum achievable non-completion return (loiter/hover for the full no-progress window + all climb reward + airborne survival) is strictly less than the return for validly completing the course. A test asserts `max non-completion return < completion return` under the shipped constants. (Preserves UC-37 AC-6 / UC-38 AC-6.)
6. **Floor-shortcut still loses to valid completion.** A trajectory that dives through the floor to reach the finish still scores strictly worse than a valid completion (it eats the — now smaller — genuine collision penalty and never earns `completion_bonus`). The reduced penalty MUST remain large enough (and/or the curriculum's late value high enough) that this UC-03 ordering holds. A test asserts it with the shipped constants.
7. **Survival ordering preserved (hover > sit).** Taking off and staying airborne over a window still returns strictly more than sitting on the floor for the same window (UC-38 AC-5). A test asserts it.
8. **Anti-suicide guarantee.** A full hovering/flying episode must return strictly more than a take-off-then-immediately-crash episode, so the policy is never incentivized to end an episode early via a deliberate crash. A test asserts `return(hover-for-episode) > return(takeoff-then-immediate-crash)` under the shipped constants. (Guards against the failure mode a flat grounded penalty would create.)
9. **Determinism, fixtures, docs, gates.** Same seed → identical reset/placement and identical trajectory for the airborne-crash and completion paths where the reward magnitude is unchanged; where reward-bearing golden values legitimately change because of the new terms, the affected tests/fixtures are updated/regenerated deliberately with the change explained (not silently deleted). README/docs updated to describe the climb reward and the crash-cliff relief/curriculum. `ent_coef` stays at the UC-38 value (0.01) unless the analyst justifies a further change. All three CI gates green (`uv run ruff check .`, `uv run ruff format --check .`, `uv run --extra dev pytest`).
10. **README reward table (standing convention).** The README training section MUST contain a reward table — created if absent, updated in this same change — with the columns `Action | Reward | Condition`, listing **every** reward/penalty term the env applies with its current shipped value and when it fires. This UC must add/refresh the rows for the climb reward and the (reduced/curriculum) collision penalty, and ensure the pre-existing terms are present and accurate: time penalty, airborne/hover survival bonus, per-gate bonus, completion bonus, collision penalty, obstacle penalty, and any others in `RewardConfig`. Example shape:

    | Action | Reward | Condition |
    |---|---|---|
    | Hover / airborne | +0.1 | per step while above the floor band |
    | Climb | +… | per step of upward progress toward hover height (potential-based) |
    | Landed / on floor | −0.05 | per step (time penalty; no separate grounded penalty) |
    | Gate passed | +10 / N | on a validly passed gate (normalized by gate count) |
    | Course completed | +100 | on valid all-gates-then-finish |
    | Collision (floor/ceiling/OOB) | −… | on genuine crash (reduced/curriculum per AC-3); terminates |
    | No-progress / timeout cut | 0 | penalty-free (UC-38) |

    A test (or a docs check, if the project has one) verifies the table exists with those columns and includes rows for the terms changed by this UC; the values in the table must match the shipped `RewardConfig` constants. This table is the single human-readable source of truth for reward shaping and MUST be kept in sync by any future reward change.

## Potential Pitfalls & Open Questions
- **Core rationale (credit assignment)** — the analyst should reason explicitly about whether the chosen levers make the *per-action advantage* of the initial "throttle up" actions positive (or at least non-negative) for a takeoff attempt that later crashes. This is the crux: a climb reward alone may be insufficient if the discounted −collision_penalty still dominates the return-to-go; that is why crash-cliff relief is paired with it. Quantify with the shipped γ and magnitudes.
- **Fixed reduction vs curriculum (design decision)** — a fixed lower `collision_penalty` is simplest and keeps a single env default, but a curriculum (low while learning to fly, ramping up for precision) is better ML and avoids permanently teaching sloppy flight. A curriculum needs the training loop to scale the penalty over steps and thread it into the env's reward config at rollout time — more plumbing and a wider test surface. The challenger must weigh simplicity vs quality and pin the choice.
- **Test blast radius** — reducing `collision_penalty` (if done as an env default rather than a training-only curriculum) touches every existing test that asserts the 100 value (UC-03/16/25/37/38 reward tests). Prefer a mechanism that minimizes churn (e.g. a training-time schedule leaving the env default at 100, or a clearly-scoped default change with all dependent tests updated deliberately). Enumerate the affected tests before implementing.
- **Sloppy-flight risk** — too-low a crash penalty and the drone learns to clip the floor/ceiling because it is cheap. Mitigated by the curriculum ramp and by AC-6 (completion must still beat the shortcut).
- **Interaction with UC-37/38** — the climb reward must compose with `airborne_bonus` (survival, stay up) and the decoupled stuck/timeout cut (UC-38). Climb = *get* up; airborne = *stay* up; stuck/timeout = penalty-free. Keep the three cleanly separated and their combined per-episode maximum below completion.
- **Potential-based form** — potential-based shaping (F = γΦ(s') − Φ(s), Φ = weight × min(height, target)) is policy-invariant (Ng et al. 1999), telescopes to prevent farming, and caps at the target so there is no ceiling-seeking incentive. This is the recommended form; the analyst may propose an equivalent bounded alternative if better justified.
- **Assumption** — the sim exposes altitude/height above `floor_z` per step (it does — the env already reads `state.position[2]` and `course.floor_z`), so no new sensing is required.

## Original Description
User, 2026-09-20, after pulling `main` (UC-38 merged) and retraining: the k1 run confirmed UC-38 works (`ep_rew` −105 → −5) but the drone still won't take off (`ep_len`≈101, 0% success, entropy rising again). User asked whether penalizing "−1 point per every tick landed regardless" would help, and asked for an honest judgment plus "do the adjustment you consider." Honest judgment: the flat grounded penalty (i) recreates ≈−105 via the stuck cap, (ii) spawns a suicide optimum (a −100 crash that ends the episode beats bleeding out at −1/tick), and (iii) does not touch the real barrier — the sit<hover ordering is already correct; the blocker is the genuine −100 crash cliff, which PPO propagates back onto the takeoff actions so "trying to fly" gets negative advantage. Chosen adjustment: relieve the crash cliff (fixed reduction or curriculum) + a small potential-based upward-progress reward (the positive-reward, non-farmable, non-suicidal form of the user's instinct), preserving all reward orderings.

## Clarifications
- Q: Would a flat "−1 per tick while landed" penalty help the drone learn to take off?
  A: No, not as the fix. It is directionally right that sitting should not be free, but it recreates the −105 (the stuck detector caps sitting at ~100 ticks), creates a suicide local optimum (crash −100 becomes preferable to sitting −105, and crashing ends the episode), and does not remove the genuine −100 crash that actually blocks the takeoff path. The effective fix attacks the crash cliff directly (relief/curriculum) plus a dense, potential-based upward-progress reward.
- Q: Fixed lower collision penalty, or a training curriculum?
  A: Left to the analyst/challenger to pin, weighing simplicity/test-churn (fixed) against ML quality and avoiding permanently-sloppy flight (curriculum). Either must preserve AC-5/6/7/8.
