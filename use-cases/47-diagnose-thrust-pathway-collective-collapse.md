# Use Case 47: Diagnose the throttle→thrust pathway (collective-thrust collapse)

## Summary
A read-mostly diagnostic use case (mirroring UC-40's diagnose-before-commit discipline) that characterizes why the drone never gains altitude even after UC-46 removed the tumbling. The UC-46 fresh GPU retrain (recordings confirmed on `git_sha eed5bd4`, fresh checkpoint, pybullet backend via UC-45 provenance) proved two things simultaneously: the attitude-authority curriculum works (roll/pitch/yaw commanded at saturated ±1.0 yet lateral drift ≈ 0 in all 19 sampled episodes — no tumble), and the drone still free-falls — `max z == spawn z` in every episode, and at *sustained commanded throttle 1.0 held level* it accelerates downward at ~7–8 m/s² (thrust-to-weight ≈ 0.2). The blocker is therefore the throttle→thrust pathway, not attitude. This UC builds a reproducible characterization harness that maps achieved vertical thrust/T-W as a function of collective throttle and simultaneous body-rate commands through the open-loop CTBR→RPM mixer (`ctbr_to_rpm` in `src/drone_fly/adapter/pybullet_adapter.py`, plus the `simple.py` equivalent), reproduces the recorded free-fall through `RaceEnv` (not a bare-adapter probe), and delivers a written verdict that confirms or refutes the leading hypothesis — that large rate commands desaturate the collective and collapse net lift — plus a data-grounded recommendation for the follow-up fix UC. It changes no reward, curriculum, init, mixer, or training dynamics.

## Acceptance Criteria
1. A committed, reproducible characterization harness runs on the real pybullet backend (via `/workspace/drone-fly/.venv/bin/python` with `PYTHONPATH=<repo>/src`) and, with domain randomization pinned to a deterministic seed/dynamics, emits a machine-readable table of achieved steady-state vertical acceleration (and derived T/W) versus command inputs.
2. **Baseline sweep (rpy = 0):** the harness sweeps collective throttle across [0, 1] with zero body-rate commands and reports achieved T/W, explicitly checking whether steady throttle ≈ 0.5 hovers and 1.0 climbs (the prior manual smoke's "throttle 1.0 + level climbs 0.9→9.36 m").
3. **Rate-coupled sweep (central question):** the same throttle sweep is repeated with simultaneous saturated body-rate commands (rpy = ±1.0) at authority-scale factors {0.25, 0.5, 1.0}, and the harness quantifies collective/vertical-thrust loss as a function of commanded rate magnitude — in particular T/W at throttle = 1.0 with rpy = ±1.0 versus rpy = 0.
4. **Oscillation effect:** the harness compares bang-bang throttle (0↔1 alternating each step) against steady throttle of equal mean, quantifying net lift lost to oscillation, and states whether the adapter models motor-RPM lag (thrust ∝ RPM²) or sets RPM directly per step.
5. **Secondary suspects quantified or ruled out:** the effect of action `latency_steps` ∈ {0, 1, 2} and of the UC-17 battery/thrust-impact (and damage) model within the first ~20 steps of a fresh episode are measured and reported (confirmed contributor / negligible).
6. **Telemetry tie-back:** episode_350's exact recorded action sequence (throttle + rpy, with its recorded spawn and dynamics) is replayed through `RaceEnv` on pybullet and reproduces the observed ~7–8 m/s² descent at full throttle within a stated tolerance, closing the loop with the real run.
7. **Hermetic CI guard:** simple-backend unit assertions encode the key structural relationships the harness measures (e.g. rate commands reduce achieved collective thrust in a stated direction/magnitude band) so the finding is regression-guarded without pybullet; real-pybullet results are reported but non-gating.
8. **Written verdict:** a concise findings note (in the UC outcome and/or `docs/`) states whether the collective-desaturation hypothesis is CONFIRMED or REFUTED, summarizes the measured T/W-vs-command surface, and gives a concrete, data-grounded recommendation for the fix UC.
9. **Diagnostic-only invariant:** the diff introduces only the characterization harness, its tests, and docs — no change to the reward function, UC-44/UC-46 curricula, climb-bias init, the mixer itself, or training dynamics. Full hermetic gate (ruff/mypy/pytest) stays green.

## Potential Pitfalls & Open Questions
- **Assumption** — "Achieved T/W" is measured via vertical acceleration in pybullet under gravity while commanding a level attitude (rpy = 0), since a direct thrust readout may not be exposed by the adapter; the harness reports actual attitude drift alongside so the numbers are interpretable.
- **Assumption** — Real-pybullet characterization is non-gating (CI is hermetic); only the simple-backend structural assertions gate CI. Consistent with UC-44/45/46.
- **Edge case** — Domain randomization varies mass/thrust/drag/latency per episode; the harness must fix a deterministic seed and dynamics to produce a clean, comparable surface, and separately sweep latency in AC5.
- **Ambiguity** — "Hold level" means commanding rpy = 0, not actively stabilizing attitude (there is no inner loop yet — building one is the *next* UC); the harness reports the resulting attitude so any residual drift is visible.
- **Missing input** — episode_350's spawn and dynamics are read from that recording's `meta` block (already downloaded this session; re-fetch via HTTP/curl from the Drive folder if needed, never the Drive MCP) so the replay is faithful.
- **Risk** — If the finding is that the mixer behaves *as designed* (rate channels legitimately consume collective headroom on a real quad), the UC must still NOT "fix" the mixer here; the recommendation (most likely the deferred inner-loop attitude/thrust stabilizer, e.g. gym-pybullet-drones `DSLPIDControl`) must follow from the measured surface, and any concrete mixer defect is deferred to a follow-up fix UC for clean attribution (as UC-40 fed UC-41/42).
